## 1. Reality gap — closed

Everything was originally verified against synthesized audio (sine triads plus click
tracks) and a local stack. That proved the plumbing, not the musicianship.

- [x] **Run real tracks from your library through extraction.** 12 files from
      `OneDrive\Music` went through cleanly. BPMs are musically plausible — house at
      122–124, amapiano at 110–115, afrobeats near 100 — and keys spread across the
      wheel rather than clustering on one value.
- [x] **Exercise the mp3 and aif decode paths.** Both work; ffmpeg and libsndfile in
      the image are doing their job. **m4a, aac, flac and ogg remain untested** — the
      library is 231 mp3 and 5 aif, so there was nothing to try them with. They are
      accepted by the upload endpoint and the bucket, so a codec gap would surface as
      a `failed` extraction rather than a crash.
- [x] **Sanity-checked against Serato.** The mp3s carry `TBPM`, `TKEY` and Serato's
      own `GEOB:Serato Autotags`, so no manual comparison was needed. Results across
      13 tracks:

      | | Agreement |
      |---|---|
      | **BPM** | **13/13 exact** (within 1 BPM) |
      | **Key** | 8 exact, 3 one step apart, 2 further off |

      **Tempo detection is solid** — Essentia matched the tags on every track, with no
      half/double errors at all.

      **Key detection is right or nearly right 11/13 (85%)**, and the failures follow
      the classic pattern for chroma-based detection: confusing a key with its
      relative or parallel minor/major, or landing a fifth away.

      - `DJames — Love Me`: ours 1B (B major), tag 1A (G# minor) — relative pair
      - `03 - House Track`: ours 12B (E major), tag 9A (E minor) — parallel pair
      - `Uncle Waffles — BAPHI`: ours 5A, tag 6A — a fifth apart, both minor

      Practically, a one-step error moves the harmonic score by ~0.1, which is
      tolerable. The parallel-mode error is the damaging one: 12B against 9A reads as
      a *clash* (0.15) when the tracks actually share a tonic. That is a materially
      wrong score, not a rounding difference.

      Suggestive but not conclusive: `03 - House Track` also had the lowest
      `key_strength` in the sample (0.55 against 0.68–0.93 elsewhere), so that value
      may be usable to flag doubtful keys. The other bad case scored 0.84, so it is
      not a complete filter.
- [x] **One-shots are now rejected rather than fabricated.** See below.

### Finding: one-shots produced confident nonsense

The library holds Serato sample-deck one-shots (`_Serato_\Imported\Samples`). The
pipeline analysed them as if they were tracks:

| File | Duration | Beat confidence | Reported before |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subdrop | 3.5s | 0.00 | 118 BPM, 12B |
| Siren | 6.0s | 0.00 | 163.6 BPM, 5A |
| Airhorn | 6.6s | **4.13** | 136.3 BPM, 10B |
| *real tracks* | 137–490s | 0.87–3.22 | plausible |

Confidence alone does not separate them — the airhorn scored higher than every real
track. Duration does, with a wide gap. `MIN_DURATION_SECONDS = 30` in
`backend/app/services/audio_analysis.py` now marks anything shorter as `failed` with
a clear reason instead of storing invented values. The track stays in the library and
is still loggable; it just carries no features.

### Still open from this pass

- [ ] **A 410 MB file in the library exceeds the 100 MiB upload cap**
      (`_Serato_\Recording\Serato Recording 1.aif`, a recorded set). It cannot be
      uploaded at all today. Recorded sets are v3 auto-detection territory, so this
      may not matter — but the cap should be a deliberate decision rather than a
      surprise.
- [x] **`PARTYNEXTDOOR & Rihanna — BELIEVE IT` at 80.0 BPM was not an error.** It had
      the lowest beat confidence of any real track (0.87), which usually signals a
      half-time detection — but the file's own tag also says 80, so the track really
      is 80 BPM. Low confidence flagged an unusual track, not a wrong answer. Worth
      remembering before treating confidence as a correctness signal.

## 2. Feature quality

- [x] **`danceability` does not saturate — the earlier claim was wrong.** It was
      derived from a synthetic click track, which is a pathological input: a perfect
      metronome is maximally "danceable". Across 12 real tracks the values ran
      **0.19–0.57 with none at the ceiling**, so `_DANCEABILITY_SCALE = 3.0` behaves
      sensibly on real music. No change needed; the field is honest.
- [x] **`energy` was RMS and discriminated poorly — replaced with a composite.**
      Across 12 tracks it ran **0.12–0.42**, using well under half its range, and it
      measured loudness rather than drive.

      Eleven candidate measures were computed over the whole library (235 files, 227
      usable — the 8 skips were all one-shots, which is the duration guard from §1
      earning its keep a second time). The rank correlations sorted them into three
      clusters, and **only three carry separate information**:

      | Axis | Members | Within cluster | vs. loudness |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Loudness | rms, **lufs**, loudness_vickers, flux | 0.68–0.90 | — |
      | Brightness | **centroid**, rolloff, high_band_ratio | 0.82–0.96 | −0.12 to −0.33 |
      | Activity | **onset_rate** | — | −0.03 |

      **Spectral flux is not a measure of drive**, which is the finding that changed
      the design — it correlates 0.85 with LUFS and is loudness in disguise. Dynamic
      complexity (−0.45) and loudness range (−0.28) are likewise absorbed by the
      loudness axis rather than adding to it.

      Each component is mapped over the library's 5th–95th percentile and the three
      are averaged equally. Equal weights because there is no labelled energy data to
      fit anything better — the same honesty problem as §3, and it should be revisited
      if ratings ever accumulate.

      | | min | p05 | p50 | p95 | max |
      |---|---|---|---|---|---|
      | RMS (before) | 0.033 | 0.173 | 0.290 | 0.385 | 0.499 |
      | Composite (after) | 0.000 | 0.157 | **0.544** | 0.861 | 1.000 |

      Every decile is populated (7–45 tracks), and **Spearman against RMS is 0.31** —
      it ranks something genuinely different. The biggest climbers are amapiano and
      afro remixes: busy and bright but not loudly mastered, so RMS buried them. Two
      acapellas landed at the floor without anything being told what an acapella is.

      **Averaging near-independent components re-clusters them** — the mean of three
      spread variables is not itself spread. The fix is stretching the mean over its
      own percentiles afterwards, and the bound matters: p05/p95 pinned 12 tracks at
      each end, so `_ENERGY_COMPOSITE_RANGE` uses p01/p99, which pins 3. The ordering
      is identical either way (rank correlation 1.000); only the tails move.

      Verified in-container against real files: the shipped `analyze_file` reproduces
      the offline calibration to within 0.0004. The 16 tracks in the local database
      were re-analysed through the service-role path and now span **0.217–0.909**
      against 0.124–0.369 before.

      **The calibration describes this library**, not an absolute scale. That is the
      right trade for one user, but the raw components (`loudness_lufs`,
      `spectral_centroid_hz`, `onset_rate`, plus the old `rms`) are stored in
      `structure_markers` so it can be re-derived from stored rows rather than by
      re-running extraction over 235 files.

- [ ] **Energy overlaps tempo more than is ideal.** `onset_rate` correlates 0.52 with
      tagged BPM, so the composite correlates 0.47 with it — a fast track scores as
      more energetic partly *because* it is fast, and BPM is already a separate stored
      feature. Musically defensible and left in deliberately, but it means energy and
      tempo are not independent inputs if anything ever consumes both.

## 3. Compatibility scoring is unvalidated

The weights and thresholds in `backend/app/core/compatibility.py` are conventional DJ
heuristics I chose, not measured values:

| Constant | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `HARMONIC_WEIGHT` / `TEMPO_WEIGHT` | 0.6 / 0.4 | tempo is correctable mid-mix, key is not |
| `TEMPO_PERFECT_PERCENT` | 3.0 | roughly what a pitch fader absorbs |
| `TEMPO_USABLE_PERCENT` | 8.0 | judgement |
| `DOUBLE_TIME_TOLERANCE` | 0.06 | judgement |
| harmonic relation scores | 1.0 / 0.9 / 0.85 / 0.6 / 0.5 / 0.15 | conventional wheel rules |

- [ ] **Tune these against your own logged combos.** Every combo you rate is a labelled
      example: if 4- and 5-star transitions cluster below the score the rules predict,
      the weights are wrong. This is also exactly the feedback signal the v2
      recommender needs (design doc §5), so the logging is not wasted either way.

## 4. Verification gaps

- [ ] **The hosted database has never had the functional RLS tests run against it.**
      The SQL is byte-identical to what passed 15 checks locally, including the
      two-user isolation tests, so there is no specific reason to doubt it — but
      `set_session_tracks` is `SECURITY INVOKER` and carries real authorization logic.
      Needs the project's `service_role` key and creates throwaway users.
- [x] **CI runs on push and pull request** — `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, three jobs:

      | Job | Covers | Time |
      |---|---|---|
      | Backend | ruff lint, format check, 257 tests on Python 3.11 (matching the image) | ~23s |
      | Frontend | `npm ci`, `tsc -b`, 127 tests | ~23s |
      | Migrations | `supabase db reset` — applies all seven migrations from scratch | ~3m |

      The migrations job is the slow one and the one that earns its keep: the RLS
      policies once shipped without table GRANTs, which every unit test passed
      straight through. Only applying the SQL to a real Postgres catches that class
      of bug. Drop it if CI minutes become a concern, knowing what is being given up.

      The typecheck step is not redundant with the tests: the vitest/vite version
      clash that broke this build once was a type error, not a failing assertion.
- [ ] **Frontend tests are entirely mocked.** That is the same blind spot that hid the
      missing GRANTs and the CORS startup crash — three of the four real bugs in this
      build were invisible to unit tests and only appeared when something real ran.
      The new migrations job closes the database half of this; the browser half is
      still uncovered. One end-to-end test (Playwright, or a committed version of the
      throwaway scripts used during the build) would cover the seams.

## 5. Missing operations

- [x] **Delete endpoints exist** — `DELETE /tracks/{id}`, `/combos/{id}`, `/sessions/{id}`.
      Deleting a track removes its Storage object too, row first so the failure mode is
      an orphaned object rather than a row pointing at audio that no longer exists.
      Verified against the live database: deleting a track cascades to its features,
      the combos referencing it, those combos' notes, and its setlist entries;
      deleting a session takes the setlist but leaves the music alone.
- [x] **Retry exists** — `POST /tracks/{id}/extract` resets the row to `pending`,
      clears stale values, and re-queues. It works from any state, since re-analysing
      is also how a track picks up an improved pipeline; only a job already
      `processing` is refused, to avoid racing the one in flight.
- [x] **UI wired up.** The track page has "Re-analyse" (disabled while a job is
      running) and "Delete"; combos have a delete control; sessions can be deleted from
      the list. All destructive actions go through a shared confirm dialog that names
      the consequence — deleting a track reports how many logged combos will go with
      it, and the session dialog says explicitly that the music is not affected.
- [x] **Standalone combo list built** — `/combos`, paired track cards with rating,
      technique and notes, delete behind the shared confirm dialog.
- [ ] **No edit for a logged combo** — wrong rating or technique means deleting and
      re-logging.
- [ ] **No session rename.** Deleting one now works; renaming does not.

## 6. Robustness

- [ ] **Extraction runs on `BackgroundTasks`**, so a job dies with the worker process
      and nothing retries it. Fine at personal scale (build spec §5 says so
      explicitly) — but see §7: deployment is what turns this from theoretical into a
      recurring failure, because every deploy and every idle-stop kills in-flight
      jobs and leaves rows stuck in `processing` with nothing to notice.
- [ ] **Essentia starves the API while it runs.** Observed, not predicted: a 30-second
      request timeout expired mid-extraction on a 5½-minute track. It runs in a
      threadpool, but holds enough CPU to make concurrent requests crawl. Harmless
      with one user and one upload at a time.
- [ ] **Upload buffers the whole file in memory**, capped at 100 MiB. The documented
      alternative is a signed upload URL with a direct browser → Storage PUT; the
      object key layout stays the same either way.
- [ ] **Essentia is pinned to `2.1b6.dev1389`.** Every published build is a
      pre-release, so the pin is exact on purpose — installing without it drags alpha
      builds of fastapi, pydantic and supabase into the image. Revisit deliberately,
      not by accident.

## 7. Deployment — parked deliberately

Assessed 2026-07-26 and postponed. The code is ready: both suites pass, CI is green,
the image builds, the production bundle works. What is unresolved is infrastructure,
and two of the constraints decide whether it costs money.

**Worth asking first: does it need deploying?** For a single-user tool, running
`docker compose up` on the laptop is a legitimate end state. Deploying buys phone
access at a gig and durability if the laptop dies — real, but they are the reason,
not "because it is finished".

### Blockers

- [ ] **Hosted database is two migrations behind.** `20260726090000` (duration and
      genre) and `20260726140000` (tag features) are local-only. One `supabase db push`
      fixes it.
- [ ] **The library does not fit Supabase's free tier.** 235 files, **1.85 GB** at
      8 MB average, against 1 GB of free storage. Either upload a subset or move to
      Pro ($25/month). Egress matters too: re-analysis downloads every file, so a full
      re-run is ~2 GB against a 5 GB/month allowance.
- [ ] **Essentia needs a real instance.** Decoding a 6-minute track to mono float32 at
      44.1 kHz is ~63 MB of samples before Essentia's working buffers, and uploads
      buffer up to 100 MB in memory. The 512 MB free tiers on Render and Railway are
      marginal to OOM-prone; want **≥1 GB RAM**.
- [ ] **`BackgroundTasks` becomes a real problem in production** (§6). Any host that
      restarts on deploy or scales to zero on idle will kill extraction mid-job, and
      nothing retries or notices. Mitigations cheapest first: pick a host that does not
      idle-stop; add a startup sweep resetting stale `processing` rows to `pending`;
      or move to a proper queue.

### Mechanical, once the above are decided

- [ ] CORS is hardcoded to `localhost:5173` — needs the deployed origin.
- [ ] No signup UI, so the hosted user must be created via the dashboard or admin API.
- [ ] Service-role key must live only on the backend host, never in a frontend build.
- [ ] No error tracking or structured logging beyond stdout.
- [ ] **Frontend bundle is 544 kB (156 kB gzipped)** in one chunk. Fine for personal
      use; code-splitting is there if it ever matters.

## 8. Smaller UX items

- [ ] **Energy is computed but barely surfaced.** It appears as one stat on track
      detail and nowhere else — the Library table sorts on BPM, key, genre and
      duration only. That was a reasonable omission while energy was RMS and meant
      little; now that it spreads across its range and ranks something real (§2), a
      sortable column and a filter are the obvious next use. Noticed while rewriting
      the README, which had claimed the column already existed.
- [ ] **Genre is only read on upload.** The 16 existing tracks show `—`; re-analysing
      backfills BPM and key tags but genre is set at upload only, so it stays empty for
      anything already in the library. ~72% of the library has a usable genre tag.

- [ ] **Reorder uses buttons, not drag-and-drop.** Accessible and testable as-is. The
      backend primitive is already "replace the whole ordered list", which is exactly
      what a drop handler needs.
- [ ] **The track picker is not a full ARIA combobox** — no arrow-key navigation
      through results, no active-descendant. Fine with a mouse.
- [ ] **Mobile layout** — *deferred by design* (build spec §3).

## 9. Still-open product questions

From design doc §9, unresolved:

- [ ] **Personal-use-only vs eventually shareable.** Every table already carries
      `user_id` and RLS is enforced, so multi-user is possible without a schema
      redesign — but it decides whether collaborative filtering is ever worth building.
- [x] **Which DJ software — Serato.** The library has a `_Serato_` folder with its own
      crates and analysis. That makes the deferred import path concretely possible
      (Serato stores BPM and key in its database and in ID3 tags), and it gives a
      second opinion to check Essentia's key detection against.
- [x] **Resolved: store both, score on the tag.** `bpm_tag` and `key_camelot_tag` sit
      beside the derived columns, and generated columns resolve which wins
      (`coalesce(tag, derived)`), so scoring, sorting and display cannot drift apart.
      Tags are read at upload, which means a track is scoreable before Essentia
      finishes; extraction re-reads them, which made the existing retry endpoint the
      backfill.

      This keeps the design doc's premise intact rather than abandoning it: extraction
      remains the only source for the ~quarter of a library with no key tag and for
      anything unreleased, and the disagreements stay visible instead of being
      silently resolved.

      **Correction to the figure this document gave earlier.** Backfilled across the
      real library, **13 of 16 tracks carry a key tag and 5 of those disagree — 38%,
      not 15%.** The lower number counted only the two cases wrong enough to flip a
      verdict; the other three are one step apart and move a score by about 0.1. Both
      are true, but 38% is the honest answer to "how often do the two sources differ".

      | Track | Analysed | Tag |
      |---|---|---|
      | DJames — Love Me | 1B | 1A |
      | PARTYNEXTDOOR — BELIEVE IT | 3B | 1A |
      | 03 - House Track | 12B | 9A |
      | Fully Focus — Oleku | 11A | 12A |
      | Uncle Waffles — BAPHI | 5A | 6A |
